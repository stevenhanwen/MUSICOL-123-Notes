# Good Neighbor Policy
- History of U.S. intervention and occupation in Mid- and South-American countries
- Official start of “non-intervention” policy in 1933
- Led by Franklin D. Roosevelt
	- Led a propaganda campaign to pull out of occupations and government intervention to foster economic trade. 
	- made travel very cheap and easy between southern neighbors and U.S.
- Trade compensation during Great Depression
- Ally development and resource management during WWII
- Millions of immigrants came to places like NYC, bringing their culture

# ASCAP Boycott of 1941
- American Society of Composers, Authors, and Publishers
- “Performing Rights Organization” or PRO
	- protects performance rights
	- ex: radio station broadcasts an artists music, artist needs to be compensated
- Non-profit establishment
- Copyright Act of 1909 added performance rights
- Est. 1914 to enforce intellectual property and copyrights
- Covers performance rights and collects royalties
	- Radio, streams, venues, clubs
	- Only one of a few kinds of royalties an artist can collect

- ASCAP received 5% of all radio broadcast revenue before 1940
- Raised demands to 15% in 1940
	- radio industry boycotted this
	- ASCAP felt they could do this because they had a monopoly of artists
- Radio broadcasters created Broadcast Music, Inc. (BMI), their own PRO
	- ASCAP turned down people who were not mainstream and classical (countries, western, blues, foreign)
- Also protested by boycotting ASCAP-protected music
- Increased airtime for regional styles (country and western), R&B, and foreign music.
	- Thus on the radio, people suddenly only heard these types
- ASCAP eventually settled on 2.8% royalty rate
	- Other music had already gained popularity and latin craze began

# The Latin Craze: 1940-60s
Mambo is a fast-paced Latin American music genre and dance style that originated in Cuba in the late 1930s and 1940s
- Grew from the danzon-mambo (popular in Cuba in the 1930s)
	- ballroom dance style. 
- Extended big band instrumentation
	- Timbales, congas, guiro, (more) guitar, maracas
	- 
- Machito and his Afro-Cubans
	- Directed by mario Bauza
	- Raised in Cuba, moved to NYC in 1937
	- "Tanga,” 1937
	- Palladium Ballroom
		- popular night club
	- National radio broadcasts
		- built into the ballroom
		- broadcast all over the U.S.

# Pérez Prado and Benny Moré, “Locas por el Mambo,” ca. 1948
- Pérez Prado
	- Blended elements of danzón with swing
	- Gained attention in Cuba and then Mexico primarily through cinema
	- Film success during Mexico’s “Golden Age of Film”
- Benny Moré
	- Expert in soneo (vocal improv in son Cubano)
- Acclaimed Dancers
	- Tongolele, Ninón Sevilla, Amalia Aguilar

# João Gilberto, “Chega de Saudade,” 1958
- Brazilian Samba + American Jazz + Refined Guitar
	- Riding the wave of the Mambo craze
- First use of bossa nova rhythms on recording
	- Related to Cuban clave
	- [Clave demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Htz94NNaUfo) (8 min)
- High fidelity mic’ing techniques
- Easy listening and Muzak
- Gilberto grew up with the sounds of the Samba, but was drawn to American smooth jazz. Was also watching the success of the Cubans. This came together to be Bossa nova.
