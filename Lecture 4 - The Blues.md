
# Characteristics of Tin Pan Alley Songs
- Voice and piano (often rearranged for bands)
- AABA is most common form
	- Also contrasting verse chorus
- Single melodies
	- Modest range
	- Basic harmonies
	- clear hooks and repeated phrases
- Danceable straight-forward rhythms
	- sometimes light syncopation
	- typically 4/4 with occasional 3/4 waltzes

# History of the Blues
- Origins in musical practices of West Africa 
- Same timeline as Tin Pan Alley
- Field Hollers, Work Songs, Ring Shouts
	- Important characteristics
		- Call-and-response
		- syncopation
		- bent notes
		- extended vocal techniques
		- improvisation
		- Melisma
		- 12-bar Blues (draws from call-and-response)
		- Topics exploring loneliness, poverty, loss, etc. 
- Performed primarily by traveling musicians
	-  After emancipation, musicians would travel with instruments that were easy to carry around - guitar. This is how Blues came to rise. 
- most prominent in the south
- moved north with the great migration
	- 1910-1970
- This is music that expands on big ideas
	- lamenting death, loss, usually sad topics
	- lingering on big ideas 

# Blue Notes
- When play scales, we hear major or minor scales
- In Blues, its a pentatonic scale
- Lowered notes added to major or minor scales
	- Usually the 3rd, 5th, or 7th

# "Cross Road Blues" - Robert Johnson (1937)
- "King" of the Delta Blues
- Scant historical record
	- Myth building after his death
- Diverse musical influences
- Guitar Techniques
	- Two-guitar illusion
	- strumming + fingerstyle
	- Hammer-ons, pull-offs, slides
- Good example of 12-bar blues

	- Marketed to black audiences

# Bessie Smith "Downhearted Blues" 1923
- Started as traveling musician, then worked up to music halls
- highest-paid Black woman singer of the time
- First sales under "race record" category at Columbia
- Cover of song written by Lovie Austin and Alberta Hunter in 1922
- Recorded with pianist Clarence Williams 
- 12-bar blues with intro verse
	- The introduction does not feel or work like a 12 bar blues
