# Popular Music Before Tin Pan Alley 
- Minstrelsy
	- medicine shows
	- blackface minstrelsy
		- People darken skin on face and then use exaggerated behavior to mock. 
	- These were traveling shows. It was a variety of shows with also juggling, dances, skits. Comparable to SNL. Typically outdoor shows from town to town. 
- Music Hall
	- Light opera
		- Sometimes were small productions like broadway. Tight relationship between broadway and light opera. 
		- Middle class to higher class
	- Vaudeville
		- Precursor to broadway. 
		- Took minstrel show and dressed it up and put it on stage. For a more respectful audience. 
		- Skilled and trained actors. 
		- Took songs from traveling minstrel shows 
		- Largely an urban phenomenon: NY, Chicago, etc. 
		- Mid 1800s
- Ragtime
- Sentimental Songs


## Buffalo Gals, Credited to John Hodges (1844)
- published in 1844
	- blackface performer, John Hodges
	- Lubly Fan - mocking Black American dialect
- Likely adapted from traditional air
- Popular Vaudeville song
- Most likely he was just the first person to claim credit of the song
- Simple verse chorus
	- music repeated between verses and choruses. 
	- Lyrics alternate new/repeated
- Voice, Banjo, guitar, violin
- Don't usually hear percussion with early vaudeville tunes

## Maple Lead Rag, Scott Joplin (1899)
- Ragtime is exclusively on the piano and performed in concert halls
- Scott was trained in European tradition, composed rags, operas, and ballet
- He blended popular and classical
	- syncopation, catchy melodies, danceable
	- Highly structured, long form, all piano
- Most popular in central U.S.
- AABBACCDD
- There are vernacular aspects, more swingy and danceable. sticky melodies
- The classical aspects are highly structured. Taken from marching band music. 

## Just a Wearyin' For You, Carrie Jacobs-Bonds (1901)
- History
	- piano playing was associated with young women and motherhood
	- amateur piano play was associated with young women 
	- So people wrote songs that women would want to play. 
	- These are sentimental songs and marketing is obvious designed for females. 
- Performed in music halls
- Enjoyed by amateur pianists
- Intended for audience of young women

# Parlor Music
- Consisted of all popular forms
	- sentimental songs
	- minstrel songs
	- music hall
	- light classical
- Performed by amateurs
- In the home
- Drive the sheet music industry
- Parlors was specific part in the home
- Typically played by families in spare time. 
- This is where Tin Pan Alley made its money

# Birth of Tin Pan Alley
- Copyright Revision of 1831
	- First protections for musical compositions - specifically for printed music
	- Before this, copyright wasn't really protected for printed music
	- A composer could say this is my song and needs to credit them
- End of Civil War - 1865
	- New economic possibilities occured
- Growth of Rail System
	- First transcontinental railroad completed 1869
	- connected east coast and west coast for better distribution. 

# The industrialization of Music
- NYC ~ 1885
- W 28th St, between 5th and 6th Ave
- Publishers
	- Feist, York, Von Tilzer
	- handled distribution of music
- Songwriters, 
	- Irving Berlin, Cole Porter
	- Just churned out songs
- Pluggers
	- George Gershwin, Al Sherman
	- go out to place with pianos and play songs in public spaces to promote them
- Vaudeville (later broadway) Song Pipeline
- Enormous business
- Questionable song quality
- Music critics thought these music were low quality and sounded the same.
