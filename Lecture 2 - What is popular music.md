**Cultivated Music**:
Griot music - west africa, japanese court music, hindustani and carnatic music in India, Beijing opera, Maqam-based music: middle east, Turkey, North Africa
- held in elite cultural status above something else. 
- Highly valued in a culture
- requires formal training
- associated with royalty, relgiion, ritual or wealth, 
- considered sophisticated, complex, and profound
- Sacred, courtly

**Vernacular Music**
- not considered a representative of the values of a culture
- does not require formal training
- associated with commoners
- considered simple and vulgar
- secular (non-sacred), common

### What is popular music

- Minstrels

	- Travelling musicians and story tellers
	- involved with the church, courts, and commoners

### Vernacular Music in Colonial America
- ~1600 - 1783
- Secular music was either discouraged or illegal
- Instrumental music not permitted in church
- Issue of documentation
- Broadside Ballads
	- Cheap, single sheet printed with song lyrics
- Songbooks
	- Bay Psalm Book (1640)
	- Usually sacred songs, but also new or collected secular songs. 


William Billings, "Chester" (1770)
- Published in 1770 in The New England Psalm Singer
- Anthem: short, simple, good for group singing
- "Chester" is the name of a town, nothing to do with lyrics
- Popular during the American Revolution
- Chester became an inspring song that became a character of the American Revolution
- Paul Revere was an engraver for the New-England psalm-singer
