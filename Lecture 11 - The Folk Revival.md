# Folk before the 1950s
Primarily during the great depression
- Important Figures:
	- Lead Belly (Huddie Ledbetter)
	- Woody Guthrie
		- Would put stickers on his guitars that protested against certain politics
		- rural working class farmer and migrant working
	- Pete Seeger
		- influenced and friends with Woody Guthrie
		- the son of charles seeger, who was a musicologist,
		- was well off
		- descended from music royalty
- Government Support
	- Works Progress Administration (WPA)
		- largely socialist and progressive
	- Raised awareness: Black and rural music
	- Became tied to liberalism and activism
- The Progressive Movement
	- American socialism
	- Anti-racism
	- Anti-militarism
	- Anti-capitalism
	- Unionization and labor rights
	- Environmentalism

# Folk Music and the McCarthy Era
- ~1947 - 1954
- Intense anti-communist paranoia fueled by senator Joseph McCarthy
- HUAC
	- House Un-American Activities Committee
- Blacklisted and jailed influential figures
- Sent the folk scene underground
	- still active in small "Bohemian" pockets like Greenwich Village in NYC.
- This all made folk performance hard in public
	- there is a decade where we don't see any of this music

# The Almanac Singers, "Which Sider Are You On?" 1941
- Written by Florence Reese, 1931
	- Sam Reese and the Harlan County War
		-The Harlan County War, or Bloody Harlan, was a series of coal industry skirmishes, executions, bombings and strikes (both attempted and realized) that took place in Harlan County, Kentucky, during the 1930s. The incidents involved coal miners and union organizers on one side and coal firms and law enforcement officials on the other.
- Studio Album: Talking Union
- Included Woody Guthrie, Pete Seeger, and many others
- Banjo and voices
- Chorus Song
	- Derived from call and response
	- Short narrative verses, solo
	- Declarative choruses, group
