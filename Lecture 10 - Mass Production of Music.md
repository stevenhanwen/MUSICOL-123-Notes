The Brill Building model was an assembly-line approach to pop music production where teams of professional songwriters, publishers, and arrangers worked in cubicles to churn out chart-topping hits. 

# Phill Spector: The Wall of Sound
- Producer for several labels
	- later started "Philles Records" with Lester Sill
- "The Wrecking Crew"
	- The Wrecking Crew was a loose collective of American session musicians based in Los Angeles who played on many studio recordings in the 1960s and 1970s, including hundreds of top 40 hits, but rarely credited at the time
	- large session band - freelance players hired to perform music
	- unique instrumentation with lots of doubling (or striping, or quadrupling)
- The Wall of Sound
	- a revolutionary music production formula from the early 1960s that treated the recording studio as a massive, dense orchestral instrument.
	- Tape primarily used in studios
	- mixed down to mono
	- recorded in small, ceramic room
	- dense, rich, complex
	- high volume
	- relaxed, yet slick
	- every instrument had their own microphones, but sounds wouldn't be protected from each other. Sounds would be layered on top of each other. 

# The Ronettes "Be My Baby" 1963
- Classic Example of the early wall of sound
	- cited by many, like Brian Wilson
- Simple, repetitive structure
- typical brill building song
- Kept interesting by virtuosic vocal performance by 
	- Ronnie Spector
- Rich, dense orchestration
- Extended with the drum solo and reprise
