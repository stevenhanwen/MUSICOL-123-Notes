# Regional Styles
- Refers to white, rural musical practices
- Country: music of Appalachia 
- Western: stylized music of the American west/southwest
- Country & Western: post-WWII consolidated sound from Nashville

# Country (Hillbilly)
- Song collecting, 1900s-1930s
	- people became interested in collecting folk songs. 
	- Became a collectors item for women or in urban areas. 
- Local Markets
- Ralph Peer
	- Producer and talent scout
	- Initial field recordings
		- Old Joe Clark by Fiddlin' Powers and Family 1925
	- Bristol Sessions, 1927
		- Big Bang of country music
		- Recording for Victor Talking Machine Company
		- Recorded both The Carter Family and Jimmie Rodgers
		- Reached national market

# The Carter Family, "Can the Circle be Unbroken" 1935
- Example of Hillbilly/Country
- First recorded by Ralph Peer
- Form: Simple Verse-chorus
- 2/4 time (with dropped beats)
- Contains instrumental verses
- "Carter Scratch"
	- Melody played on low strings
	- Harmony played on high strings

# Western
- Hollywood portrayal of "Cowboy Music"
	- Gene Autry, Roy Rodgers
	- Primarily recorded in California
- Western Swing (country jazz)
	- grew out of Great plains
	- combination of western and big band
	- Forth Worth, Texas was a hub by 1930s
	- Bob Wills and HIs Texas Playboys

# Bob Wills and His Texas Playboys, "San Antonio Rose", 1940
- Example of Western Swing
- Use of steel guitar by Leon McAuliffe
- Combination of urban and rural aesthetics
	- Big Band: extended harmonies in piano solos, dance band rhythm sections
	- Country: old-time style diddle and vocal harmonies
	- Western: lyrical and visual themes
	- Polka: "oom-pah" sound throughout

# Jimmie Rodgers
- First country music start
- Moved between country and western
- "Yodeling Cowboy"
- Ramblin man persona
- He was actually very wealthy, not a hobo/hillbilly
	- Very characteristics which made him popular
- He had very good vocals. 

# The Nashville Sound
- Jimmie Rodgers and boundaries between country and western
- Grand Ole Opry (Nashville) & National Barn Dance (Chicago)
	- Radio broadcasts that specialized in country and western
- Impacts of WWII
- Nashville publsihers abandon sheet music
	- Acuff-Rose and Castle Records
	- In house songwriters. 

# Hank Williams, "Hey Good Lookin" 1951
- Words and Music by Hank Williams
- Form: AABA (repeated)
- Straight duple
- Characteristic of the Nashville Sound
	- No drums
	- 2-step rhythm
	- steel guitar
	- Folksy, "authentic" vocals
- Cheeriest Williams Song

# Patsy Cline, "I Fall to Pieces" 1961
- Recorded after her first contract expired
	- #1 in C&W, #12 in pop chart
- Backed by the Jordanaires
- Country pop sound
	- Silk production
	- 2-step (but with percussion)
	- pop-inflected vocals
	- honky-tonk piano and some steel guitar
- Laid groundwork for women in Nashville

# Country and Western vs Mainstream Vocal Timbres
**Country/western**
- open
- no vibrato
- "calling"
- unrefined
- earnest and untrained
- emotitional
- no moaning/growling (Blues has it)
**Mainstream**
- Smooth
- vibrato
- closely mic'd
- refined
- cool and almost operatic
- intimate
- no moaning/growling
