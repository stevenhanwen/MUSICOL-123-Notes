Sound-related elements: Pitch, volume, timbre
time-related elements: Rhythm, meter, tempo
Organization-related elements: Melody, harmony, texture, form

Pitch: 
- highness or lowness of sound - has to do with the frequency
- higher pitch has higher frequency
Volume
-  loudness
- Measured by amplitude of sound wave
Timbre
- Color or unique characteristics
- Tone
- Sometimes difficult to describe

# Musical Application
- Pitch letter names (A-G)
- Sharps (#) and Flats (b)
	- Half steps on keyboard
- Interval 
	- Distance between two notes
- Range
	- Distance between highest and lowest notes on an instrument or in a song

# Time-Related Elements
Rhythm 
- How note lengths are organized; ordered flow of music through time
Meter
 - Organization of beats into regular units
	- (Beat: regular, steady pulse that divides music into equal units of time)
- Basic Types of Meter: duple, triple, compound
Tempo
- How quickly or slowly beats move

# Organization-Related Elements
- Melody
	- Pitches organized in a sequence
	- Move in steps and leaps
	- Can be legato or staccato (smooth or detached)
- Harmony
	- The way multiple pitches work together; organization of chords
	- Chord: pitches that sound together
	- Consonance vs dissonance
		- More stable and “pleasant” versus clashing

## More on Harmony
- Scale: Set of notes used to form a melody
- Key: The scale used to write a song
	- Generally major or minor
- Modulation: Changing keys within a song/section of a song
	- Whitney Houston, “I Will Always Love You” (1992) 1:44, 3:07
		- Direct modulation: A to B
	- Avenged Sevenfold, “Beast and the Harlot” (2005) 4:42
		- Pivot modulation: F to A

# Organization-Related Elements (cont’d)
- Texture: How many and what kind of instruments you hear at one time
	- Luke Combs, “Better Together” (2019): Thinner texture – only piano and vocals
	- Vildhjarta, “toxin” (2021): Thicker texture – multiple guitars, bass guitar, drums, vocals, synths, piano:
- Form: The way sections in a piece of music are organized
	- A-section, B-section
	- Verse, Chorus, Pre-Chorus, Bridge
