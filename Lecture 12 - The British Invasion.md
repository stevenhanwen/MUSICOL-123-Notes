# Origins of the Beatles
- 1957: John Lennon and Paul McCartney
	- Skiffle band called the “Quarry Men”
- 1958: George Harrison joins
- 1960-1962: Played shows in Hamburg and Liverpool
	- First recordings with Tony Sheridan
	- “My Bonnie”
- June 6, 1962: First recording
	- “Love me Do” with Pete Best drumming
	- Met George Martin at EMI Parlophone

# Rhythmic Characteristics
- Double clap
	- Syncopated hand clapping
	- Common in girl groups
	- “Please, Mr. Postman,” The Marvelettes, 1961
	- “My Boyfriend’s Back,” The Angels, 1963
- Mersey Beat
	- Derived from polka -> country & western
	- Emphasis on the “backbeat”
	- Typically in snare drum

# Best vs Starr
“George Martin was used to drummers being very 'in time', because all the big-band session drummers he used had a great sense of time. Now, our Liverpool drummers had a sense of spirit, emotion, economy even, but not a deadly sense of time. This would bother producers making a record.

George took us to one side and said 'I'm really unhappy with the drummer. Would you consider changing him?' We said 'no, we can't!' It was one of those terrible things you go through as kids. Can we betray him? No. But our career was on the line. Maybe they were going to cancel our contract.”

Paul McCartney, 2014

# Leading up to the Invasion
- Oct. 1962: EMI (Parlophone) releases "Love Me Do"
- Jan. 1963: Capitol refuses Beatles license, Vee Jay signs them
- Feb. 1963: Vee Jay releases "Please Please Me"
- Sept. 1963: Black Swan releases "She Loves You"
- Dec. 26, 1963: Capitol releases "I Want to Hold Your Hand"
- Feb. 1, 1964: "I Want to Hold Your Hand" tops US charts
- Feb. 9, 1964: The Beatles come to US and appear on Ed Sullivan


# “I Want To Hold Your Hand,” The Beatles, 1963
- Charted before The Beatles came to the U.S.
- First release with a U.S. major label
- AABA-derived form
	- Chorus+Verse
	- Contrasting Verse
- Mersey beat

Beatles:
- Stopped performing live, 1966
- Broke up, 1969

# British Blues Revival and Tale of Two Bands
The Beatles:
- Pop-based rock
- Based in Liverpool
- Cute, funny public image
- Long hair
- Suits and ties
- Struggled to get signed
The Rolling Stones:
- Blues-based rock
- Based in London
- Rough, bad boy image
- LONGER hair
- Suits and ties (quickly discarded)
- Signed with EMI immediately

# The Rolling Stones, “Satisfaction”
- British Blues Revival
	- Blues records were trendy collectors’ items
	- Blues-style guitar very popular
- Sound inspired by Muddy Waters and Electric Blues
	- Distorted guitar sound
	- Rough vocals
- Combined electric blues and pop influences
- NOT 12-bar blues
- Contrasting verse-chorus
- 4/4 throughout, driving snare on each beat
