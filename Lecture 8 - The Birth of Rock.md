
# The 1950s: What's Going On?

- Communist "threat"
- post-war prosperity
- advances in civil rights and access to mass media
- growth of market segmentation
- Involved parenting extended youth and these kids often had access to mass-produced record players and records
- Youth with disposable incomes
- children with different values than parents
- Teens viewed as new, important market

# DJs and Their Influence
- Disc Jockeys as arbiters of taste
- Alan Freed out of Cleveland in 1951, then NY in 1954
- Dewey Philips, "Red, Hot, and Blue" out of Memphis
	- R&B shows, they got airtime in the evenings. 
	- stations would also be mainstream stations but only during after hours
	- during this time, a kid could have a radio in their room, and listen without their parents knowing. 
- William Perryman "Doctor FeelGood" out of Atlanta, Al Benson out of Chicago

# Labels and Payola
- The Majors: Decca, Mercury, RCA-Victor, Columbia, Capitol, MGM
	- specialized in mainstream pop
- Indie Labels: everyone else
	- Okeh, Black Swan, Chess, Atlantic
	- Specialized in Country & Western and R&B
	- Benefitted from payola practices. This is just bribery. 
	- Indies had trouble getting their records played by radio stations. They had to aggressively market to the radio stations. 

# Crossover
- Crossover: when a song from one chart gains popularity on another chart
- Charts are based on presumed tastes of target audiences
- Two kinds of crossover
	- One recording in both charts
	- Song covered in both charts
- Publishers held rights to song lyrics
	- Royalty incentive for multiple recordings of the same song
	- It was about which recording of the song people liked best.

# Covers
- Cover: When an artist records a new version of another artist's song
- White artists frequently covered R&B singles sung by Black artists
- publishers encouraged the practice

Fats Domino, "Aint that a Shame" vs. Pat Boone "Aint that a Shame"


# Chuck Berry, "Maybellene" 1955
- Adapted from “Ida Red” by Bob Wills and His Texas Playboys
	- “Polka” two-step from Western Swing
- Hard driving rhythm section
- Virtuosic blues-inspired guitar
	- Double stops in guitar solo
	- Influenced by Delta Blues-style playing
- Raucous performance
	- (both vocally and physically)
- Lyrics and style directed at teen audiences
- it is a 12 bar blues
	- the lyrical pattern is not
	- the echos have 12 bar blues

#  Little Richard, "Tutti Frutti" 1955
- Simple verse chorus
- 12-bar blues influence in verses
- Boogie-woogie derived piano
- Vocal performance influenced by gospel and blues
- Flamboyant stage styling
	- “Wild man” persona made it difficult for him in the pop market

# Bill Haley, "Rock Around the Clock", 1954
- Gained popularity through use in Blackboard Jungle
- Became anthem for youth rebellion
- No indication that it is a cover (although this is still contested)
- Clean cut, but energetic vocal performance
- Literally caused riots
